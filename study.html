The study

Aims, research questions, participants, design, procedure and ethics.

Aim

To measure how well undergraduate students know APA 7th edition referencing rules, to identify the rules they most often get wrong, and to explore how students believe academic writing has influenced their critical thinking skills. The findings will be used to design short, targeted APA support materials for students.

Research questions

RQ1

How accurately do undergraduate students apply APA 7 rules for in-text citations and reference-list entries?

RQ2

Which APA rules are most often misunderstood, and does accuracy differ by year of study or prior APA instruction?

RQ3

How confident are students in their APA skills, and does confidence match their actual test score?

RQ4

How do students describe the influence of academic writing on their critical thinking skills (analysing, evaluating, inferring, explaining, self-regulating)?

Design

A convergent mixed-methods design: a quantitative survey (knowledge test + rating scales) and qualitative semi-structured interviews are carried out in the same period, analysed separately, and then compared.

Participants

  • Sample size: 50 undergraduate students.
  • Sampling: convenience sampling through course groups; participation is voluntary.
  • Inclusion criteria: enrolled undergraduate; has written at least one assignment that required references.
  • Profile collected: year of study, field of study, prior APA instruction (yes/no), main language of academic writing. No names or ID numbers.
  • Breakdown by year of study: [DATA NEEDED] (see the Results page).

Procedure

  1. Information and consent. Students read the information sheet and give consent on the first page of the survey.
  2. Survey (about 15 minutes). Sections A–D, completed without notes, books or AI tools, so that the test measures what students actually know.
  3. Interview invitation. At the end of the survey, students can volunteer for an interview using a code word, not their name.
  4. Interviews (30–40 minutes). Audio-recorded with permission, transcribed, then the recordings are deleted.
  5. Analysis. Survey data are summarised; interview transcripts are coded; the two strands are compared.
  6. Feedback to students. Participants receive a short summary and the APA guide on this site.

Ethics and privacy

  • Participation is voluntary and has no effect on grades; students can withdraw at any time before analysis.
  • No names, ID numbers, grades or e-mail addresses are collected in the survey.
  • Interview quotations are reported under pseudonyms (e.g. "Student 4"), and details that could identify someone are removed.
  • No personal data is entered into AI tools at any stage.
