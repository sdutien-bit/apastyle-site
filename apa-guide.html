1. Why reference?

  • Give credit to other people's ideas — using an idea without a citation is plagiarism, even if the words are your own.
  • Help readers find your sources.
  • Show the evidence behind your argument.

2. Two parts, one rule

In the text

A short pointer inside your sentence: author and year.

Example

Sleep affects memory and attention in students (Walker, 2017).

In the reference list

The full details, at the end of your paper.

Example

Walker, M. (2017). Why we sleep: Unlocking the power of sleep and dreams. Scribner.

The rule: every in-text citation has an entry in the reference list, and every entry in the list is cited in the text. The surname and year in the text lead the reader to the same entry in the list.

3. In-text citations: two styles

Parenthetical

The idea comes first; the source is in brackets at the end.

One author

Memory improves after sleep (Walker, 2017).

Two authors

Memory improves after sleep (Smith & Lee, 2020).

Narrative

The author is part of your sentence; the year follows in brackets.

One author

Walker (2017) argues that memory improves after sleep.

Two authors

Smith and Lee (2020) found a similar effect.

Remember: use & inside brackets and and in the sentence.

  • In APA 7, use "et al." from the first citation when there are three or more authors.
  • Abbreviate a group author only if you will use the abbreviation again.

5. Paraphrase and quotation

Paraphrase

  • Use your own words and your own sentence structure — not just a few swapped words.
  • Author and year are required.
  • A page number is encouraged but optional.
Example

Memory improves after sleep (Walker, 2017).

Short quotation

  • Fewer than 40 words.
  • Quotation marks and a page number.
  • p. 3 for one page; pp. 3–5 for a range.
Format

“Quoted words go here” (Walker, 2017, p. 3).

The format is shown with placeholder words, not a real quotation.

Block quotation

  • 40 words or more.
  • Start on a new line and indent the whole block 0.5 inch.
  • No quotation marks.
  • The citation goes after the final full stop.

Block quotes are rare in student papers — paraphrase where you can.

6. Special cases

No date

Write n.d. (no date).

Parenthetical

(Okafor, n.d.)

Narrative

Okafor (n.d.) explains how to plan an essay.

Secondary source ("as cited in")

You read Smith's idea in Lee's book and could not get Smith's original.

In the text

(Smith, 2010, as cited in Lee, 2020)

Only Lee (2020) goes in your reference list, because that is the book you read. Try to find the original first; use "as cited in" only when you cannot.

Several sources for one idea
Example

(Adams, 2018; Brown, 2020; Chen, 2019)

Alphabetical order by first author (not date order), separated by semicolons.

Same author, same year
Example

(Adams, 2020a) and (Adams, 2020b)

The letters follow the order of the titles in your reference list, and the same letters are added to the year in the list.

7. Reference list rules

  • Start on a new page with the heading References, bold and centred.
  • Order entries alphabetically by the first author's surname. Ignore "A", "An" and "The" when alphabetising titles.
  • Use a hanging indent of 0.5 inch (in Word: Paragraph → Special → Hanging → 0.5").
  • Double-space the whole list.

9. Anatomy of a reference: four building blocks

Every reference answers four questions: Who? (author) When? (date) What? (title) Where? (publisher, journal, website, DOI or URL).

Book

Jackson, L. M. (2019). The psychology of prejudice: From attitudes to social action (2nd ed.). American Psychological Association. https://doi.org/10.1037/0000168-000

1 · Who2 · When3 · What4 · Where

If a reader can't find the source from your reference, the reference isn't finished.

Showing 11 examples

  • Book
    Reference list

    Jackson, L. M. (2019). The psychology of prejudice: From attitudes to social action (2nd ed.). American Psychological Association. https://doi.org/10.1037/0000168-000

    In the text

    (Jackson, 2019) or Jackson (2019)

    • Title in italics and sentence case.
    • Edition in brackets: (2nd ed.).
    • Add the DOI if there is one.
    • No city of publication in APA 7.

    Example from the APA Publication Manual, 7th ed.

  • Journal article
    Reference list

    Grady, J. S., Her, M., Moreno, G., Perez, C., & Yelinek, J. (2019). Emotions in storybooks: A comparison of storybooks that represent ethnic and racial groups in the United States. Psychology of Popular Media Culture, 8(3), 207–217. https://doi.org/10.1037/ppm0000185

    In the text

    (Grady et al., 2019) or Grady et al. (2019)

    • Article title: sentence case, no italics, no quotation marks.
    • Journal name and volume in italics.
    • Issue number in brackets, not italic: 8(3).
    • Page range with an en dash: 207–217.
    • Comma before & when there are three or more authors.

    Example from the APA Publication Manual, 7th ed.

  • Chapter in an edited book
    Reference list

    Balsam, K. F., Martell, C. R., & Safren, S. A. (2006). Affirmative cognitive-behavioral therapy with lesbian, gay, and bisexual people. In P. A. Hays & G. Y. Iwamasa (Eds.), Culturally responsive cognitive-behavioral therapy: Assessment, practice, and supervision (pp. 223–243). American Psychological Association. https://doi.org/10.1037/11433-009

    In the text

    (Balsam et al., 2006) — cite the chapter authors, not the editors.

    • Chapter authors first.
    • Editors after “In”, initials first; (Ed.) for one editor, (Eds.) for several.
    • Italicise the book title, not the chapter title.
    • Add the page range: (pp. 223–243).

    Example from the APA Publication Manual, 7th ed.

  • Web page — individual author
    Reference list

    Hartmann, J. (2022, March 3). Sleep and study habits: What students should know. Student Wellbeing Hub. https://example.org/sleep-study

    In the text

    (Hartmann, 2022)

    • Give the full date if the page shows one.
    • Italicise the page title.
    • Website name comes after the title.

    Practice example (invented for teaching).

  • Web page — group author
    Reference list

    World Health Organization. (2020). Physical activity. https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity

    In the text

    (World Health Organization, 2020)

    • When the group is both author and publisher, write the name once.
    • Write the group name in full in the reference list, not the abbreviation.
    • Check the date on the live page before citing.

    Reference list

12. Common errors: spot the mistakes

Incorrect

Smith, John. 2020. Learning To Teach. London: Oxford University Press.

Show the five errors and the correct version
  1. Full first name instead of an initial.
  2. No brackets around the year.
  3. Title Case instead of sentence case.
  4. Title not in italics.
  5. City of publication included (not used in APA 7).
Correct

Smith, J. (2020). Learning to teach. Oxford University Press.

Practice example (invented for teaching).

Also watch for: missing hanging indent, wrong order, "and" instead of "&" in the list, and citations with no matching entry.

13. Final checklist

In the text

  • Author and year for every source
  • Page number for direct quotations
  • "et al." for three or more authors
  • Every citation matches a reference

In the reference list

  • Alphabetical order, hanging indent
  • Author, date, title, source
  • Sentence case; italics in the right place
  • DOI or URL where available
