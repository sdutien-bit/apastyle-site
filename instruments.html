Information and consent (survey page 1)

This survey is part of a student research project about APA referencing and academic writing. It takes about 15 minutes. Taking part is voluntary and has no effect on your grades. We do not ask for your name, student ID, grades or contact details. Please answer Section B without notes, books or AI tools — we want to know what students really know, not what they can look up.

☐ I have read this information and agree to take part.

Interview guide: academic writing and critical thinking (14 questions)

Format: semi-structured, one-to-one, 30–40 minutes, audio-recorded with permission. About 10 volunteers from the 50 survey participants.

Before starting: repeat the consent information; remind the student that there are no right or wrong answers and they can skip any question; ask them not to name teachers or classmates.

Badges show which critical thinking skill each question mainly explores (used for coding).

A. Opening

  1. Can you tell me about the academic writing you have done at university so far — what kinds of assignments, and which one you are most proud of? Warm-up

    Probe: Which subjects? How long were the texts? Did they require references?

  2. What does “critical thinking” mean to you as a student? Warm-up

    Probe: Can you give an example from your own studies?

B. Sources and evidence

  1. How do you decide whether a source is reliable enough to use in an essay? Evaluation

    Probe: Tell me about a time you decided NOT to use a source. What made you reject it?

  2. Has learning to reference in APA style changed the way you read and choose sources? If so, how? Evaluation

    Probe: Do you now look at the author, date or journal before reading? Why?

  3. When two sources disagree with each other, what do you do in your writing? Analysis

    Probe: How do you decide which one to follow? Do you mention both?

C. Building an argument

  1. How do you build the main argument or thesis of an essay? Has your way of doing this changed since your first year? Inference

    Probe: What do you do differently now? What caused the change?

  2. How do you deal with counter-arguments or evidence that does not support your position? Analysis

    Probe: Can you give an example from a recent assignment?

  3. When you paraphrase or quote a source, how do you connect it to your own ideas? Explanation

    Probe: How do you make sure the reader can see where the source ends and your own argument begins?

D. Revision and reflection

  1. How do you use feedback from teachers or classmates on your writing? Self-regulation

    Probe: Describe one piece of feedback that changed how you think, not just how you write.

  2. Has writing an essay ever made you change your opinion on a topic? What happened? Self-regulation

    Probe: What evidence or step in the writing process caused the change?

  3. How do you check your own work before you submit it? Self-regulation

    Probe: Do you use a checklist? Do you check that every citation matches a reference?

E. Tools and integrity

  1. Do you use citation managers or AI tools when you write or reference? How do you check that their output is correct? Evaluation

    Probe: Have you ever found an error or a source that did not exist?

F. Closing

  1. Overall, which part of academic writing has helped your thinking the most, and why? Interpretation

    Probe: What would you tell a first-year student about this?

  2. Is there anything else about academic writing, referencing or critical thinking that you would like to add? Closing

Scoring and analysis plan

  • Section B: 1 point per correct item; total 0–15. Bands: 0–5 low, 6–10 medium, 11–15 high. Item difficulty = % of the 50 students who answered correctly.
  • Sections C and D: mean (and standard deviation) for each item and for each section.
  • Confidence vs. knowledge: Spearman correlation between the Section B total and the Section C mean.
  • Interviews: transcribed and analysed with thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke, 2006), using the six critical thinking skills as starting codes and adding new codes where needed.
